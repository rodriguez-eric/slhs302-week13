Could one real-world sound give the two ears different inputs?

Check Your Understanding

What changes when the right-ear tone flips, and why can that lower threshold?

Reveal answer

The right tone’s peaks become troughs, while the noise waveform and the tone level at each ear stay fixed. The resulting difference between the ears can help the auditory system separate the tone from the masker.

Check Your Understanding

What does the BMLD mean in this example?

Reveal answer

52 − 41 = 11 dB. The listener detected the opposite-phase tone at a level 11 dB lower with the same masker.

Left–right movement can change both interaural timing and level. Front–back judgments can also use direction-dependent spectral cues. A sound growing louder can suggest approach if its source level is stable.

Check Your Understanding

What cues could make a sound seem to move around you?

Reveal answer

Timing and level differences can signal side-to-side movement. Changes in the spectrum can help with front–back judgments, and rising level can suggest approach if the source level stays steady.

Check Your Understanding

Why can 0° and 180° sound alike in this model?

Reveal answer

Both are on the midline and produce equal timing and level at the two ears in this model. Real listeners can also use pinna filtering and changes caused by head movement.

What are you holding equal in this task?

Reveal answer

Perceived loudness. Two tones at different frequencies can require different playback levels to sound equally loud.

Check Your Understanding

If two tones match in pitch, must they also match in loudness?

Reveal answer

No. Pitch describes how high or low a sound seems. Loudness describes how strong it seems.

Check Your Understanding

Why can you still hear a pitch near 160 Hz after that component is removed?

Reveal answer

The remaining 320, 480, and 640 Hz components are still spaced 160 Hz apart. Their combined waveform still repeats every 6.25 ms.

Move the filter across a 160 Hz harmonic series. Notice what happens as the illustrative filter bandwidth grows with center frequency.

Why are higher harmonics harder to resolve?

Neighboring harmonics stay 160 Hz apart. Higher-frequency auditory filters are wider in Hz, so several harmonics are more likely to fall within one passband.

Check Your Understanding

Why can several higher harmonics fall in the same filter?

Reveal answer

The harmonic spacing stays at 160 Hz in this example, while the auditory filters get wider in Hz at higher center frequencies.
